# Societal Guide — The Six Societies (Public)

Consolidated reference for the six intelligent-life societies — profiles, habitat/spawn logic, and the full relationship wheel in one document. Source material: [[03 Active Projects/Xander's Game/Design/Public/Master Design Document (Public).md]] → "The Six Societies" and "The Two Fundamental Forces"; [[03 Active Projects/Xander's Game/Design/Public/Planetary Generation System (Public).md]] → "Society habitat requirements."

## Overview

| Society | Nature | Alignment | Relative rarity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Humanoids** | Natural human-like settlements | Yang | "Default" relatable society — not rare |
| **The Industry** | Robots/AI lifeforms | Yin-leaning, "not powerful" | Common wherever resources justify it |
| **The Cult** | Rare, small settlements, magical/advanced | Yin worshippers | Rare by design |
| **The Bugs** | Large organic colonies, farming-heavy | Yang-leaning | Common |
| **The Mushrooms** | Tiny hive-minded mass, barely noticeable | Both Yin and Yang | Most common lifeform in the universe |
| **The Network** | Single mechanical hive-mind, acts as one unit | Opposes both Yin and Yang | Placement/construction not yet designed |

## Profiles

### Humanoids
- Natural settlements, Earth-like/organic planets. The "default" relatable society.
- **Alignment: Yang.**
- **Habitat:** organic-forming composition (C/H/O/N present) strongly preferred; breathable atmosphere strongly preferred (toxic survivable only with life support); ~1g preferred, tolerates 0.5–1.5g.
- **Survivability/settlement-scale rules (the real spawn-probability driver):**
  - **Frozen/Scorching bands:** require high technological complexity and large-scale settlements to survive at all — low spawn probability, and a large *abandoned* settlement in these bands is exactly what a failed high-tech colonization attempt would look like (ties to the "abandoned = small, or large-and-destroyed" distribution rule).
  - **Cold/Hot bands:** small-and-surviving or large-and-thriving both possible, but never at Individual Scale — no lone wandering NPCs or unprotected single buildings; minimum viable presence is a real Operational Scale Site with life support.
  - **Temperate band (or an artificial dome-simulated one):** the *only* condition where Individual Scale presence — lone NPCs, tiny unprotected settlements — is possible at all.
- **Rare hostile-environment outlier, worked example:** Humanoids in an artificial habitat (life-support bubble) on an airless silicate world — see the Containment Collapse worked example in the Planetary Generation System doc for the actual subsystem-failure mechanism.
- **Real alliance: Humanoids ↔ Bugs, mutual** — see the Bugs' profile below for the reasoning (farming/labor collaboration between the two Yang-camp organic societies).

### The Industry
- Robots/AI lifeforms. Desolate, resource-dense, harsh planets. Ugly machinery aesthetic, heavy space presence.
- **Alignment: Yin-leaning, "not powerful."**
- **Habitat:** metal/silicate-rich composition, especially high-value ores — otherwise indifferent to temperature/atmosphere/gravity (mechanical, unaffected by extremes). **Resource-value-driven placement, not habitability-driven** — presence is about whether a planet has what Industry wants, not whether Industry can "live" there.
- **Real alliance: Industry ↔ Cult, mutual.** Industry handles materials in abundance but not rarity or magic; the Cult's spell technology lets Industry hunt down additional resources and perform conversions it couldn't manage on its own. Genuine complementary need, not a one-sided favor — see the Cult's profile below for what Industry provides in return.
- **Motivation — proposed, not locked:** deliberately *not* framed as "AI is inherently cold." Instead: The Industry is still executing an ancient founding directive (a "Prime Quota" or similar) from whoever built it, with no revision mechanism ever added — millennia later, still blindly optimizing for an order nobody's given in ages. Indifference as *stuck automation following outdated orders*, not an inherent trait of machine minds. Sets up a clean contrast with the Network (see below): Industry = rigid, ancient-directive-bound, no real unifying awareness; Network = actively adaptive, genuinely unified hive-mind.

### The Cult
- Rare, small settlements, magical/advanced. Split off from the Humanoids and became their mortal enemy — an ideological schism turned bitter rivalry.
- **Alignment: Yin worshippers.**
- **Habitat:** exotic/rare minerals a draw, otherwise flexible; favors extreme temperature bands (Frozen/Scorching) for isolation value, congruent with Yin alignment; flexible atmosphere/gravity via tech or magic-assisted survival. Isolation- and rarity-seeking over comfort.
- **Character note:** given their rarity, default disposition toward other societies is neutral-unless-needed, not proactive alliance-seeking — situational, not a standing warm relationship even where positive.
- **Real alliance: Cult ↔ Industry, mutual — the concrete exception to that aloofness.** The Cult deals in magic, spells, and exceptionally rare materials, but not infrastructure or scale — Cult uses Industry's infrastructure to house members in the harshest climates (Frozen/Scorching bands), which happen to already be exactly where both societies independently operate. In return, Industry gets access to Cult's spell technology for resource-hunting and material conversions. A real trade of complementary strengths, not charity in either direction.
- **Real asymmetry: Cult → Bugs stays Neutral, purely extractive.** The Cult uses Bugs as a resource for spell materials and provides no major benefit in return — see the Bugs' entry below for how the Bugs themselves misread this relationship entirely.

### The Bugs
- Large colonies, farming-heavy. Can build hives from raw materials even in space. Not violent unless provoked; self-sustaining (animal+plant as one kingdom, feeds off itself) — an organic hive-swarm archetype, but calmer and less violent than the genre norm.
- **Alignment: Yang-leaning.** Organic themselves, but without Humanoid-level complex thought. Build according to Yang principles (won't destroy life/things unnecessarily) but pragmatically "march over anything in their path" when needed — the same practical indifference as Industry, despite the philosophical alignment difference. **Less calm, more assertive than the Mushrooms.**
- **Habitat:** organic-forming composition preferred, tolerant of toxic-organic conditions (e.g. sulfurous swamp worlds); warm-to-temperate preferred, tolerant of Hot; doesn't need breathable atmosphere; flexible, low-moderate gravity preferred. Presence scales with organic abundance.
- **Real rivalry: Bugs regard the Industry as an enemy.** Reasoning: the Industry are "stronger and more organized lifeforms of a similar variety to the Bugs" — more resilient to the Bugs than the reverse — "and the bugs are aware of this flaw in their own design." A real, acknowledged design weakness the Bugs are conscious of, not a mutual grudge (Industry's stance back toward Bugs stays Neutral — the threat isn't reciprocally felt).
- **Real mistaken alliance: Bugs → Cult Friendly, but not reciprocated.** The Bugs aren't intelligent enough to fully distinguish the Cult from Humanoids — they read both as the same underlying lifeform and disregard the philosophical split between them, wrongly assuming the Cult treats them with the same benefit-giving relationship the Humanoids do. In reality the Cult → Bugs relationship stays Neutral and purely extractive (spell-material resource use, no real benefit given back) — a real one-sided misunderstanding baked directly into the relationship data, not just flavor text.
- **Real alliance: Bugs ↔ Humanoids, mutual.** Humanoids value the Bugs' large-scale farming/labor capacity — straightforward, uncomplicated collaboration between the two Yang-camp organic societies, no stated friction anywhere in it. This is the Yang side's mirror to the Yin side's Cult↔Industry alliance below.
- **Industry regards the Bugs' cousins, the Mushrooms, as Friendly** — not the Bugs themselves, but worth noting the pattern: Industry finds practical, resource-driven use in both the Bugs' domain (farming) and specifically the Mushrooms ("could use mushrooms in farming and transforming materials"), even while the Bugs and Industry themselves remain adversarial. Consistent with Industry's general resource-value-driven placement logic.

### The Mushrooms
- Tiny, hive-minded mass, barely noticeable until it "presents itself." Requires almost nothing to live.
- **Alignment: Both Yin and Yang.** At the center of both life and death — decomposers, resetting the life cycle as bottom feeders. Population scale makes total elimination effectively impossible. **Leans notably more Yang than Yin in practice** (reflected in the tilted relationship-wheel divider — a real asymmetry, not a perfect 50/50 split), which may represent "the philosophical limit of the game" if narrative material ever gets built around it.
- **Habitat:** minimal requirements, near-universal; wide tolerance on temperature (avoids only Scorching extremes), atmosphere, and gravity. The baseline lifeform, present in small numbers almost everywhere.
- **Relationship to everyone: Neutral across the board.** Worth keeping visible rather than treating this as permanently locked — this is one of the design's own acknowledged fundamental-limit decisions, still being tested rather than closed.
- **Why, philosophically:** the Mushrooms feed off dead and inanimate material *in service of life* — decomposers moving the dead back into the living. Because their function is to convert death into life, they need to be neutral to both sides of that exchange, while genuinely **favouring life when needed**, "as it is a force that moves the dead into the living." Their Yang lean (see the tilted relationship-wheel divider) is a direct consequence of this function, not an arbitrary stat. See "Life/death philosophical framework" below for the full picture, including the Network's mirror-opposite role.
- **A striking consequence:** **4 of the other 5 societies (Humanoids, Cult, Bugs, Industry) all independently value the Mushrooms positively** — while the Mushrooms stay neutral back to every one of them. The only relationship that isn't warm toward the Mushrooms is the Network's. This pattern wasn't designed top-down; it fell out of individual calls made across several separate design passes.
- **The wheel's overall shape:** two internal alliances now sit on either side of the Yin/Yang divide — Humanoids↔Bugs (Yang side) and Cult↔Industry (Yin side), both mutual — alongside every non-Network society independently valuing the Mushrooms. The Mushrooms sit as the one relationship point every camp agrees on without reciprocating; the Network sits as the one point nobody's allied with at all. A wheel with two cooperative camps, a shared point of quiet reverence, and one true outsider.
- **Speculative, uncommitted idea:** "Elder Mushrooms" or an ancient mycelium-network interface, possibly tied to a specific mushroom planet — a rare centralized point of mushroom intelligence, floated as the *only* mechanism through which the Mushrooms' otherwise-universal neutrality could ever flip to active enmity toward anyone. Not designed.

### The Network
- Single hive-minded mechanical presence, acts as one unit.
- **Alignment: opposes both Yin and Yang** — a genuine third position, not aligned with either fundamental force. **A totalitarian singularity that mimics Yin's own nature**, ironic given its ideological opposition to Yin.
- **Story hook, not committed:** if narrative elements get written, Yin may be the one to eventually crush the Network.
- **Relationship to everyone: Neutral across the board, at least outwardly** — mirrors the Mushrooms as "the most direct equivalent to Yang and Yin respectively." Same still-being-tested caveat applies as noted under Mushrooms above.
- **Why, philosophically:** the Network consumes the living, and is inherently dead — though *not* inanimate. The direct mirror-opposite of the Mushrooms' function: where Mushrooms convert dead→living, the Network converts living→dead. This is the real grounding for its Yin lean. Consistent with the Mushrooms/Network texture below: mechanically Neutral, but the Network is the one side of that pairing that's actually encroaching.
- **The Mushrooms/Network relationship carries real narrative texture underneath the flat Neutral label:** the Network *does* observe the Mushrooms, and — accepting it is far smaller and younger than them in its current state — spends its time trying to expand and catch up to their scale, **cutting the Mushrooms down as it goes.** A worldly equivalent of the Yin/Yang story arcs. Quietly adversarial in practice despite the mechanical Neutral rating.
- **Habitat/placement/construction: fully deferred.** Its conditions are different enough from the other five that designing this now, before its visual identity exists, would be premature. Revisit once its look and spawn behavior are further along.

## Life/death philosophical framework
Real thematic architecture, not just flavor text — grounds *why* the Mushrooms and Network are universally Neutral rather than it being an arbitrary simplification:

- **Mushrooms** feed off dead/inanimate material in service of life — "a supporter of life" through death, converting the dead back into the living, favouring life when their function calls for it.
- **The Network** consumes the living and is inherently dead, though not inanimate — the mirror-opposite conversion, living→dead rather than dead→living.
- **This frames the living organic societies (Humanoids, Bugs, Cult) as a kind of middle ground** within the setting's philosophy, with "dead" itself acting as a conduit between two extremes: **inanimate unexistence** on one end, **complete wholistic infinity** on the other.
- Still actively being developed — treat it as a live thread, not a finished cosmology.

## The Two Fundamental Forces (context for Yin/Yang alignment above)
Not good/evil, not a Western dualism — "beings which possess typically different features, though with some overlap." Working names, placeholders.

- **Yin:** enormous, edgeless creature — a face floating in the emptiness of space, possibly spawning from a black hole; controls a floating white hand puppeting a jeweled celestial dummy. Visual direction: sharp, triangular natural-rock-formation features, and an ornate, otherworldly boss-creature design language drawing on classic platformer final-boss silhouettes. Likely final boss. Reward: a weapon. Occupies the universal origin coordinate — player spawn is deliberately far away, so finding Yin is a late-game emergent discovery, never signposted.
- **Yang:** lives at Individual Scale — a bright shining humanoid, wise, mildly magical, never abuses its power. Found only in untouched wilderness, reached on foot and unarmoured. Reward: a tool.

## The Relationship Wheel

![[03 Active Projects/Xander's Game/Design/Public/assets/relationship-wheel.png]]

**Model:** directional/asymmetric (Society A's stance toward B ≠ B's stance toward A, in general), 3 states — **Friendly** (a general positive characteristic to the relationship, not necessarily a battle-pact), **Neutral** (covers both situational neutrality and genuine indifference), **Enemy**. Layout deliberately echoes the Yin/Yang taijitu: true opposites sit across the hexagon from each other (Mushrooms↔Network, Humanoids↔Cult, Bugs↔Industry), with the dividing line tilted so Mushrooms lands on the Yang side and Network on the Yin side, rather than a forced even split.

**Yin and Yang have real nodes on the chart:** both sit on the pure horizontal line through the hexagon's true center, symmetric left/right, in close toward the middle. Styled distinctly (gold-bordered) to read as forces rather than societies.

**Relationship design finalized — all lines rendered fully opaque.** "Finalized" refers to the relationship *values* — the Reasoning column below still has genuine gaps, filled in incrementally rather than all at once.

## In-world physical rendition — idea, parked
The exact chart (the hexagon, the arrows, the Yin/Yang placement) is intended to physically exist somewhere *inside* the game itself — discoverable, not labeled or explained. Candidate forms floated: a rune etched somewhere in a cave, a constellation visible in the sky, possibly sited near the universal origin (Yin's location). Ties naturally to the discovery-over-instruction design pillar — the player would have to actually recognize what they're looking at, with no UI callout confirming it. **Not designed — parked for whenever Individual/Operational Scale content-building starts for real.**

## Status
**Relationship design finalized** — all values dictated, one asymmetric mistaken-alliance case (Bugs→Cult) and two mutual alliances (Humanoids↔Bugs, Cult↔Industry) added on top of the original pass, all lines now fully opaque, Yin/Yang given their own chart nodes. Includes a philosophical grounding for why the Mushrooms and Network are universally Neutral (see "Life/death philosophical framework" above) — this framework is still being actively tested, not permanently locked, preserved rather than smoothed over. The Network remains the one society with no habitat/placement logic at all. Reasoning column intentionally has real gaps — narrative "why" for each relationship is being filled in incrementally, not all at once.
